# Market Research Standards

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. National, provincial/state, and municipal government statistics and regulatory pages
2. Official industry regulators, standards bodies, and trade associations
3. Company websites, filings, catalogues, and official pricing pages for competitor facts
4. Credible academic, bank, economic-development, and established research publications
5. Reputable business media for recent developments

Use aggregator or directory data only as a lead and corroborate material claims. Do not cite search-result snippets.

## Jurisdiction and currency defaults

When the jurisdiction is unspecified, use Canada and express every monetary figure in CAD. Prioritize:

- Statistics Canada and the Canadian Industry Statistics portal
- official NAICS Canada classifications
- Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada
- Bank of Canada for macroeconomic context
- Canada Revenue Agency for federal tax information
- the relevant province, municipality, regulator, and licensing authority
- local economic-development and census-profile sources for customer geography

When the client clearly operates in another country, or the user names one, switch the whole engagement: research that jurisdiction's statistical agency, industry classification system, tax authority, and regulators, and express every figure in that jurisdiction's currency. State the jurisdiction and currency convention prominently in the plan and the workbook Source Log. A client with cross-border operations gets one working currency, chosen with the user, with the choice recorded as a decision.

Whichever jurisdiction applies: when a useful source reports money in another currency, convert it using an authoritative exchange rate, state the rate and its as-of date, and preserve the original figure in the source note. Match the date and geography of data to the client's target market, and state clearly when only national or older data is available.

## Required research record

For each material fact, capture: the claim or model input; value and units; geography and period; source organization and page title; direct URL; access date; and any limitation or transformation applied.

Put full citations in the plan's source appendix. Put financial-input sources in the workbook's Source Log with matching assumption IDs.

## Market sizing

Prefer bottom-up calculations:

`reachable customers x expected penetration x annual purchase frequency x average price`

Use TAM, SAM, and SOM only when the definitions are explicit:

- **TAM:** all demand in the broad relevant market
- **SAM:** demand within the business's product, geographic, and channel constraints
- **SOM:** realistic share reachable during the forecast period

Show the formula and assumptions. Reconcile the SOM to forecast revenue or explain why they differ. Avoid unsupported market-share claims.

## Competitor analysis

Identify three to six meaningful competitors when available. Include direct competitors, indirect competitors, and substitutes. Compare evidence-based dimensions such as customer, offering, price, location or channel, convenience, reputation indicators, and differentiators.

Do not state a competitor's weakness as fact without evidence. Frame unverified observations as implications or hypotheses.

## Regulatory research

Identify potentially relevant licences, zoning, tax, employment, privacy, accessibility, environmental, food or health, import/export, and professional rules for the applicable jurisdiction. Link to the responsible authority. Describe requirements as items to verify when applicability depends on unresolved client facts. Do not provide legal conclusions.

## Scenario grounding

While researching, note the two or three quantified risks most likely to move the forecast: demand softness in the sector, input-cost inflation, wage pressure, seasonality, regulatory timing. These become the named drivers of the downside and upside scenario cases, so record the figures that justify the driver magnitudes.

## Freshness and uncertainty

- Use the current date as the research date.
- Prefer the latest available official release and record its reference period.
- Distinguish current facts from projections.
- Say when reliable local data is unavailable.
- Use ranges or scenarios when uncertainty is material.
- Never invent citations, sources, prices, competitor details, or regulations.
