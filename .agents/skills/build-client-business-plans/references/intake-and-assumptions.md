# Hybrid Intake and Assumptions

## Purpose

Ask the smallest set of questions needed to prevent a misleading plan or unusable forecast. Extract answers from all supplied files before asking anything.

## Essential-question gate

Ask only questions that remain unanswered in these areas:

1. **Identity and jurisdiction:** business/trading name, operating location, new or existing status, and legal form if known. Location settles the research jurisdiction and currency: Canada and CAD by default, the client's own jurisdiction and currency when they clearly operate elsewhere. Ask about currency only when the jurisdiction is genuinely ambiguous (for example cross-border operations); otherwise it follows the location.
2. **Offer and customer:** specific products/services, target customers, target geography, and the primary customer problem.
3. **Revenue mechanics:** price, expected unit/customer volume, purchase frequency, launch/start date, and practical capacity.
4. **Project and financing:** purpose of the plan, amount requested, use of funds, owner contribution, and desired financing type if known.
5. **Operating costs:** major direct costs, payroll/headcount, rent, marketing, insurance, technology, and other material fixed costs.
6. **Existing-business history:** available sales, cost, profit, balance-sheet, debt, and cash information. Ask for the periods the client actually has, not an ideal data package.
7. **People and execution:** founder/management names, relevant experience, key hires, milestones, and responsibilities.
8. **Special constraints:** deadlines, lender or program requirements, required forecast horizon, or confidentiality restrictions.

Normally ask no more than eight grouped questions in one round. Combine related missing details into compact questions. Do not ask for optional details merely because a template field exists, and do not ask for anything needed only for the downside/upside cases: scenario drivers come from the research and the risk analysis, not from the client.

## Proceeding after the intake

- Proceed when identity, offering, market, timing, and basic revenue/cost logic are sufficiently clear.
- Use conservative, visible assumptions for remaining forecast gaps.
- If historical financials are unavailable, create a forecast-only draft and leave historical periods blank or label them as not provided.
- If the financing amount is unknown, calculate a preliminary funding need from startup costs and peak cash deficit, then label it as an estimate.
- If price or volume is unknown, use a researched benchmark only when a credible local or industry source exists; otherwise use a clearly labelled planning assumption.
- Ask a second question only when jurisdiction, unit economics, or financing structure remains too ambiguous to model responsibly.
- When the user is away or the run is unattended, skip the question round entirely: choose the most defensible interpretation for each gap, record every choice in the assumption register with `Confirmation needed: yes`, and open the delivery note with the interpretation taken.

## Assumption register

Record every material assumption with these fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| ID | Sequential ID such as A-01 |
| Category | Market, revenue, cost, timing, financing, tax, staffing, or operations |
| Assumption | Specific numeric or narrative assumption |
| Basis | Client statement, source benchmark, calculation, or professional judgement |
| Confidence | High, medium, or low |
| Effect | Main plan/model sections affected |
| Confirmation needed | Yes or no |

Use high confidence only for well-supported facts or mechanically derived values. Do not disguise weak assumptions with precise decimals. The same IDs appear in the workbook's Source Log so the register and the model trace to each other.

## Sensitive information

Do not request government identifiers, account numbers, card numbers, passwords, or full credit reports. Personal net-worth, birth-date, family, employer, and spouse fields in the financial template are optional. Complete them only from information the user intentionally supplies and explicitly wants included.
